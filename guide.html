Quick start

  1. Open the Studio and click a look.
  2. Open Layers, pick a layer, and move the sliders.
  3. Press the shuffle icon for a new variation of the same design.
  4. Add your name or logo in Logo.
  5. Open Export and download the print file.

Made a mistake? Undo is at the top, and your work saves by itself in this browser.

The three views

  • Flat is the print template, shown upright with the shoulder at the top and the wrist at the bottom. Switch between Front and Back at the bottom. You paint on the Front.
  • Flat-lay shows the front and back laid flat, like a product photo.
  • On arm shows it worn. Drag to turn the arm. Marks at the sides are easiest to judge with Left or Right.

Designs wrap around the sleeve. The back continues each layer past the seams, so nothing stops dead at the edge of the arm.

Layers

Looks and layers come in two sets. Hand-made is drawn with a simulated hand, with brushes, paint and wear. Classic keeps the cleaner graphic presets.

A design is a stack of layers. Add one from Add, then pick it in Layers to change it. Each layer can sit on the left, the right, or both sides of the arm.

Worn print goes on top of a design and rubs parts of it away, which makes almost anything feel older and more real.

Under Position on the arm you can limit a layer to part of the sleeve, or keep it away from the elbow.

Painting

Press Paint and draw on the sleeve. It works with a tablet pen, a finger or a mouse. Ink reacts to pen pressure where your device reports it.

  • Ink gives a line that thickens and thins.
  • Marker gives an even line.
  • Dry gives rough bristle marks.
  • Spray scatters fine dots.
  • Erase removes whole strokes.
  • Mirror sides copies each stroke to the opposite side.

Getting it printed

Arm sleeves are usually printed by sublimation, which dyes the design into the fabric. Most print shops and sportswear makers offer it.

Tips for a good result

  • Check the print check. The badge in the Studio tells you if a line is too thin or a logo is in a risky spot.
  • Mind the elbow. It stretches the most, so keep fine lines and text away from it.
  • Keep important things off the seams. Leave about 1 cm clear.
  • Allow for movement. Printed graphics can land a little off, and one printer warns of up to about 5 cm, so avoid designs that need exact placement.
  • Order one sample first. Fabric stretch is the one thing a screen can't fully show you.

Questions

Which file do I send to the printer?

Send the Print file (SVG) if they accept vector files, or the Print image (PNG, 300 dpi) if they don't. Both are full size, with the front and back side by side.

Where are my designs saved?

In the browser you used. They don't move between devices, and clearing your browser data removes them, so export anything you want to keep.

Do I need an iPad?

No. Everything except pen pressure works with a mouse. A USB drawing tablet also works on a computer.

Can I use my own logo?

Yes. Open Logo, then More options, and upload a PNG, SVG, JPG or WebP. It stays on your device.
